# Why Novosibirsk Is Dirty: the "lawn above the curb/sidewalk" mistake — causes, cost, and a citywide fix plan

> Preconstruction (presale) report. Combines 4 research streams + an independent audit.
> The report includes **only what the audit confirmed**; vendor-claims and unverified figures are explicitly flagged.
> The scale cost estimates are **NOT a bill of quantities** — they are a bottom-up order-of-magnitude with explicit assumptions.

---

## Executive summary

**The client's hypothesis is confirmed as a real, normatively documented defect — but REJECTED as the "sole/main cause" of the dirt.**

- **A lawn above the curb/sidewalk with no edge step** is a real mechanism by which soil is washed onto the pavement, directly contradicting the in-force SP 82.13330.2016 (Russian landscaping code) (the lawn must be BELOW the bordering edge) and the local Landscaping Rules. This is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition for cleanliness.
- **The problem has been publicly acknowledged by the city hall itself.** Mayor M. Kudryavtsev (23.10.2025) called the "non-standard lawn" a source of dust and described the correct solution ("a soil bed below the curb works as a rain inlet"). The city hall **already has a running program** of lawn restoration: 2024 — 3×RUB 15 mn, 2025 — RUB 135 mn, 2026 — RUB 130 mn. The client is entering an **occupied field** — differentiation must be built not on "discovering the problem," but on scale, prioritization, acceptance control, and coverage of courtyards/developers.
- **The main cause of dirt, per the only study found, is NOT lawns but the winter sand–salt mix / anti-icing materials (sand–salt mix) (~50%).** This "50%" is a verbal expert estimate (SGUPS / IPA SB RAS, D. Razuvaev), **with no methodology and no confidence interval**, so it is itself flagged as doubtful. In descending order after that: pavement wear; soil from the private sector and construction sites; exhaust ("a tiny bit"). "Lawns above the edge" is a subset of the secondary erosion/wash-off factor, not root cause #1.
- **An underrated parallel root cause is the storm drainage system.** Per the audit's verification (academ.info, city hall): in fact ~**400–420 km** of storm drains, ~**10%** territory coverage, a need of ~**RUB 44 bn** (35.2 collectors + 8.8 networks + >5 from court orders), and there is no money in the budget. Without water drainage, a sunken lawn turns into a flooded pit. Storm drainage is a **separate mega-program** capable of absorbing the entire scope.

**Scale (bottom-up) — NOT proven, order-of-magnitude.** With a road network of ~3,298 km and an unknown share of problem segments (hypothesis 20–60%), reworking "curb + lowering the lawn" across the city = **on the order of RUB 3–25 bn** (range center ~RUB 6–10 bn), an ~8× spread due to the lack of an inventory. Including storm drainage (~RUB 44 bn), the full program is **tens of RUB bn, a 10–15+ year horizon**. At the current pace of RUB 130 mn/year the lawn program would stretch over **40–190 years** — this is the main signal that a "standalone" program is unrealistic.

**Bottom-up cost is NOT proven.** All unit prices are predominantly **vendor-claim (Moscow-based)**, with a 3–6× spread; they cannot be defended as an estimate. For a budget program, calculate using FER/GESN (Russian state cost-estimate norms) with the Novosibirsk Region territorial indices.

**AI applicability — low/niche, honest verdict: NOT the core.** The program is infrastructure-and-regulation-based. AI is justified only as an optional inventory layer (computer vision over aerial/panorama imagery to detect "lawn above the edge" and exposed soil across 3,298 km) and prioritization of the worst segments. One can and should start without AI. "AI for AI's sake" is an antipattern here.

**Go/No-Go: NO-GO until blockers are closed.** First of all: the budget holder/asset custodian, the inventory (8× spread), the post-works maintenance regulation, and instrumental confirmation that the root cause is lawns, not anti-icing materials.

## 1. The problem and its causes

### 1.1. The defect mechanism (confirmed by norm and by experts)

When the lawn/topsoil surface is **above** the edge of the sidewalk or curb and there is no step, rainwater and meltwater flow "from the lawn onto the pavement," carrying soil particles; on hard pavement the soil turns to mud and is spread by wheels/shoes. The correct geometry is the reverse: the curb flush with/above the pavement, the lawn/soil a few cm lower — water flows **from the pavement onto the lawn** together with dust and sand, while the edge holds back the soil. The curb (GOST 6665-91) is a physical barrier between "carriageway / sidewalk / lawn"; when the edge is sunken or destroyed, the barrier function is lost.

### 1.2. Ranking of causes (corrected by the audit)

> **Important (a conflict between streams, resolved by the audit):** streams #1/#2/#3 put #1 = anti-icing materials (~50%), stream #4 wrongly put #1 = road wear, lawns #2. The primary source (Razuvaev/ndn.info) supports "anti-icing materials 50%." The primary source's ranking is used. The "50%" figure is itself **doubtful** (a verbal estimate, with no methodology).

| Rank | Cause | Share / estimate | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Winter sand–salt mix / anti-icing materials** (sand with clay exceeding GOST; "of a tonne of sand, ~700 kg is dust/clay") | **~50%** | Low (one verbal study, no CI) |
| 2 | **Pavement wear** (studs, abrasion, asphalt crumbling) | no exact % | Medium |
| 2–3 | **Erosion/wash-off from lawns and exposed soil** (incl. "lawn above the edge") | no exact % | Medium |
| 3 | **Soil from private-sector roads and construction sites** (no wheel washing) | no exact % | Medium |
| 4 | **Parking on lawns, "desire paths," drawn-out restoration after excavations** | qualitative | Medium |
| 5 | **Vehicle exhaust** | "a tiny bit" | Medium |

Related: **~2/3 of the city's territory is unpaved roads and exposed soil** (SB RAS/SGUPS, snippet — primary source needed); **760 km of dirt roads = 23%** of the road network, **27% of roads do not meet standards** (Novosibirskstat-2022 via sibnovo.info, medium confidence). Winter 2023/24: "Bionord" 3,880 t + anti-icing materials (sand/crushed stone) 40,820 t over part of the season (city hall via ngs.ru, high). The spring "dust smog" contains derivatives of road dust, anti-icing materials, soil particles, soot, heavy metals (PM10 — irritation of the airways, exacerbation of asthma/bronchitis in children).

### 1.3. The root of the defect is a violation of in-force norms, not their absence

> **A conflict between streams, resolved by the audit:** stream #4 uniquely claimed "SP does not forbid a lawn ABOVE the edge (a loophole)." **This is incorrect.** The SP wording "below the bordering edge by no more than 2 cm" sets a range of 0…−2 cm; a lawn above the edge is **already a violation of SP**. The correct reading (stream #2): this is **mass non-compliance with norms** and a lack of acceptance control of the lawn level, not a regulatory gap.

Why the defect reproduces: (1) there is no acceptance control of construction-and-installation works at the "lawn/edge" level; (2) soil accumulation — over years sand, leaves, and soil settle, and the layer grows above the edge without any rework; (3) exposed soil "has not yet become a lawn"; (4) utility-company excavations without restoring levels; (5) **there is no single party responsible for systemic change** ("the city hall has no people responsible for changes").

## 2. Stakeholders and financing

### 2.1. Who decides and who pays (fragmented by asset custodian)

| Stakeholder | Area of responsibility | Money |
|---|---|---|
| **City hall / Department of the Road-and-Landscaping Complex (DDBK)** | Arterials, roadside lawns, storm drains (~400–420 km) | City budget, BKD (national "Safe Quality Roads" program) / "Infrastructure for Life" |
| **GUBO / MKU "Gorzelenkhoz", districts** | Maintenance of lawns, public gardens (~240 ha on the balance sheet) | Municipal |
| **Municipal road-maintenance unit (district)** | Cleaning of district-level roads, sidewalks | Municipal |
| **Management companies / HOAs** | Near-building greening strips, courtyard lawns | Residents + FKGS |
| **Developers** | Landscaping/greening of new districts per zoning / land-use permits | Private (via acceptance) |
| **Residents** | Co-financing via FKGS / "My Courtyard" (deputies' mandates) | Voluntary |

**The root barrier: there is no single "owner of the problem."** This is precisely why the problem was not solved earlier.

### 2.2. Financing models (with an honest applicability assessment)

- **Direct budget (dominant).** The city hall's lawn program: 2024 — 3×RUB 15 mn, 2025 — RUB 135 mn, 2026 — RUB 130 mn (audit-confirmed).
- **FKGS + deputies' mandates.** Courtyards 2024: 213 territories under mandates + 30 under FKGS, RUB 560 mn total.
- **BKD / "Infrastructure for Life."** Tying the curb/lawn rework to the planned road repair is the **cheapest marginal channel** (the curb is dismantled anyway during a road repair). The Novosibirsk roads budget 2024: **RUB 7.04 bn** (federal 1.195 + regional 1.845 + city 4.001), 262 km, 2.85 mn m² of asphalt (high confidence). NB: the federal national project FKGS/"Infrastructure for Life" was being **cut** (ERZ.RF) — one cannot build a plan on growth in federal money.
- **Developer obligations (zoning / land-use permits).** Shift the correct lawn arrangement onto the developer through acceptance conditions. Requires stronger control. *An underdeveloped cheap lever (flagged by the audit as an omission).*
- **Recourse against violators (a working precedent).** Via the courts (April 2026) the city hall obliged a heat-network company to restore "asphalt, sidewalks, curbs, and lawns" at 7 sites in 2 districts (1 month + a fine of RUB 1,000/day). A ready mechanism for recovering restoration costs from those who violated levels during excavations. *Not scaled as a source of fund recovery (an omission).*
- **PPP / concession — unlikely for linear street lawns.** ROSINFRA: 37 PPP projects in landscaping (~RUB 6.9 bn) — these are parks/embankments/lighting with a revenue stream, NOT street lawns. There is no monetization for linear greening → do not budget it as a main channel.

> **Separating budget levels (an important honesty point, audit-confirmed):** **RUB 24.3 bn for 3-year road maintenance is the REGION** (TUAD, ~12,600 km of roads, 2026–29; after tenders ~RUB 21.7 bn), **NOT the city**. Do not confuse it with the city's street-cleaning budget. A separate line for "city street washing/cleaning" is not disclosed in public sources — verification is needed via the procurement of the MKU "DEU" under Federal Law 44-FZ (public procurement) (zakupki.gov.ru).

## 3. Engineering solution by element + norms

### 3.1. Regulatory basis (what is already required)

| Norm | Requirement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **SP 82.13330.2016 "Landscaping of Territories"**, cl. 9.3 | The surface of the settled topsoil layer is **below the bordering edge by no more than 2 cm** | In force (confirmed verbatim from the mos.ru PDF) |
| SP 82.13330.2016, cl. 9.37 | Dividing strips: the edge above the carriageway **15 cm**, the soil **below the edge by 5–10 cm** | In force |
| **Novosibirsk Landscaping Rules** (Council of Deputies Decision No. 469 of 27.09.2017, rev. 23.12.2019) | The road edge above the carriageway **≥150 mm**; the garden edge above the lawn **≥50 mm** over a length of ≥0.5 m | In force (local norm) |
| **GOST 6665-91** "Concrete and reinforced-concrete edge stones" | Standard sizes (BR 100.30.15 = 1000×300×150 etc.); purpose — separating the carriageway from sidewalks/lawns | In force |
| **SP 32.13330.2018** "Sewerage. External networks", sec. 7 | Design of rainwater (storm) sewerage, surface runoff | In force |
| **SP 475.1325800.2020** | Where pedestrian links meet a lawn — a garden edge with an excess over the lawn of **≥50 mm** over ≥0.5 m | In force |

**Conclusion:** the problem is enforcement and acceptance control of construction-and-installation works, not the absence of norms. The cheap systemic lever is to close the practice through acceptance and (if needed) refine the local Landscaping Rules.

### 3.2. Engineering solution by element

1. **Curb/edge.** The lawn/topsoil below the rim by 2–5 cm (SP 82 cl. 9.3); a garden edge with an excess over the lawn of ≥50 mm (Novosibirsk Rules / SP 475); restoration/replacement of destroyed and sunken edges.
2. **Lawn.** Cutting and hauling away accumulated soil, leveling, a new fertile layer, seeding or rolled turf; ground-cover plants instead of exposed soil (the city hall's 2025 practice already applies this in pilots).
3. **Water drainage.** Rain inlets + storm sewerage (SP 32) — critical: at ~10% coverage, a sunken lawn without a water inlet = a flooded pit.
4. **Biodrainage / rain gardens** (an advanced option, the lawn-as-rain-inlet): depth 15–40 cm, drainage layer of gravel/crushed stone 5–15 cm, fertile layer, moisture-loving plants; they pass ~30% more water into the soil and reduce the load on the storm drains. In Russia this is still at the pilot stage (Perm — HOAs/activists; a Krasnoyarsk administration handbook; Cherepovets). **There are no systemic SP and price norms in Russia** — the climate of Novosibirsk Region (freezing, snow accumulation, anti-icing-materials load) requires adaptation; do not transfer Western infiltration norms.
5. **Stabilization of slopes/parking areas.** Geogrid/lawn grid on slopes and parking zones, mulch/gravel for tree pits, hedges along arterials.

### 4.2. City-wide range (bottom-up, order-of-magnitude — NOT an estimate)

**Assumptions (all require verification; the main driver of the total is the share of problem segments):**
- Road network ~3,298 km; an edge on both sides ⇒ ~6,600 km of rim.
- Share of problem segments **unknown — hypothesis 20–60%** ⇒ 1,300–4,000 km of rim (≈2–3.3 mn linear m two-sided).
- Lawn strip along the edge ~1–3 m ⇒ ~1.5–12 mn m².

**Calculation:**
- Edges: 1,300–4,000 km × RUB 1,500–3,000/m ≈ **RUB 2–12 bn** (edge + dismantling + base).
- Lawns (cutting/lowering + seeding): 1.5–12 mn m² × RUB 300–800/m² ≈ **RUB 0.5–9.6 bn**.
- **Total for edges+lawns only: on the order of RUB 3–25 bn** (center ~RUB 6–10 bn; an ~8× spread).
- **+ Storm drains/biodrainage on part of the segments: up to ~RUB 44 bn** (a separate mega-block).

**Scale sanity-check:**
- The full program (edges + lawns + storm drains) = **tens of RUB bn** — comparable to the city's annual budget (~RUB 108.2 bn, expenditures 2025). Realistic only as a phased, address-specific program.
- At a pace of **RUB 130 mn/year → term ≈ 40–190 years**; even at an optimistic RUB 6 bn — ~46 years. **Conclusion: the current pace does not solve the problem within any foreseeable horizon.** The only realistic path is to embed the rework into the planned road repair (the BKD channel ~RUB 7 bn/year) + recourse against utility companies/developer obligations, not a "standalone construction."

> **Is scale/cost proven bottom-up: NO.** The numbers are an order-of-magnitude. The key blocking uncertainty is the share of problem segments (8× spread) and the lack of local estimates per FER/GESN.

## 5. AI applicability — honest verdict

> Audit: the honesty on AI across all 4 streams is exemplary, the verdict is unanimous and well-founded.

| Layer | What it is | AI? |
|---|---|---|
| **Built WITH AI** | Analytics of causes, maps of problem zones, systematization of norms, synthesis of prices, formatting the program | AI as an analyst's tool — appropriate, but this is **not a "product with AI"** |
| **AI-free CORE (mandatory, the program MVP)** | Norms (SP 82 / 475, Novosibirsk Rules), the engineering solution, estimates per FER/GESN, acceptance, financing, the maintenance regulation | **No AI needed. Classic. AI in the core = the "AI for AI's sake" antipattern → NO-GO** |
| **Optional AI module (2nd phase)** | CV inventory: auto-detection of exposed soil and "lawn above the edge" from aerial/panorama/satellite imagery across 3,298 km; prioritization of the worst segments (slope, proximity to storm drains, traffic, complaints) | **Yes, a measurable advantage — but an option, not a blocker.** HOLD until it is confirmed that the manual inventory is the bottleneck |

**AI analogs in cities (for inventory):** Moscow — a neural network searches for unwashed sidewalks, lawns, curbs, uncut grass from mobile units/cameras; Saint Petersburg's "Gorodovoy" (potholes, landscaping, trash). This solves a real bottleneck task: a manual walkthrough of 3,298 km is unrealistic.

**Verdict:** AI is justified **only** at the inventory/monitoring stage as an accelerator. Building AI into the core is premature. Recommendation: **an AI-free MVP** (regulation + manual/selective inventory on a pilot); AI-based measurement is a scaling option, a decision to be deferred until data appears. A cheaper alternative: prioritization by 2GIS complaints/citizen appeals + selective walkthrough — without AI.

## 6. CITYWIDE PLAN

**Stage 0 — Inventory and diagnostics (closing blockers).**
A survey of the length of problem rims ("lawn above the edge / no edge / destroyed edge") and the area of exposed soil (the municipal road-maintenance unit / the city's GIS, or a selective/CV inventory). Instrumental confirmation of the share contributed by "lawns" versus "anti-icing materials + cleaning" (samples, hauled-sweepings volumes). Verification of local FER/GESN prices for Novosibirsk Region and of the real estimates in the city hall's 2024–2025 contracts (Federal Law 44-FZ, the EIS zakupki). Closes the 8× spread and the question of whether the money goes "into the wrong root cause."

**Stage 1 — Pilot (2–3 "dirty" arterials + 1–2 courtyards).**
A full node: edge + lawn lowering (−2…−5 cm) + garden edge +50 mm + ground-cover plants + spot rain inlets/rain gardens. Measuring the effect (dustiness, wash-off volume, puddles). Working out the acceptance regulation per SP 82 / Rules No. 469.

**Stage 2 — Prioritization of the worst segments and scaling.**
Ranking the queues by traffic intensity / runoff / slope / proximity to storm drains / citizen complaints. A multi-year address-specific program **tied to the planned road repair (BKD)** — combine it with road-network reconstruction so as not to pay for excavation twice. In parallel: storm drainage as a separate mega-block (do not ignore it — otherwise lawns = cosmetics over an unresolved water drainage problem).

**Stage 3 — Acceptance regulation + post-works maintenance.**
Control of the steps at the handover of construction-and-installation works; developer obligations through zoning / land-use permits; recourse against utility companies for violating levels during excavations (the 2026 court precedent); a cleaning/wash-off regulation against soil accumulation (otherwise the layer will build up again over the years). Optionally — a component "anti-icing materials: sand → granite chips/reagents + wet cleaning" (otherwise the cleanliness effect is partial).

**Financing of the plan:** the municipal road fund + BKD (embedding into road repair) + developer obligations (via the Landscaping Rules) + co-financing by management companies / HOAs for courtyards + recourse against violators. Do NOT budget PPP for linear lawns.

2. **[BLOCKER] Inventory.** How many linear m of problem rims and ha of exposed soil are there REALLY? No data → an 8× spread on the estimate. The first stage and the first go/no-go.
3. **[BLOCKER] Asset custodian / single responsible party.** City/DDBK/DEU vs management companies / HOAs vs developers. There is no single "owner of the problem" ("the city hall has no people responsible for changes"). Not closed.
4. **[BLOCKER] Financing source and channel.** A standalone program (RUB 130 mn/year → 40–190 years, unrealistic) vs embedding into BKD (~RUB 7 bn/year). Determines the realism of the timelines. Not closed.
5. **[BLOCKER] Post-works maintenance.** Who, and under what regulation, prevents the soil from building up above the edge again? Practically unaddressed — a serious gap.
6. **[BLOCKER] Real estimate unit prices per FER/GESN for Novosibirsk Region** (to replace vendor-claim). Without them the citywide cost is indefensible.
7. **[BLOCKER for positioning] What exactly the client is "selling"** against the backdrop of the city hall's already-running program: scale? coverage of courtyards/management companies/developers? an acceptance-control system? AI monitoring? Without this the presale is empty.

**Recommendation:** GO on the **AI-free core (Layer 0)** after closing blockers 1–4. **HOLD on the AI module (Layer 1)** until blocker 2 is answered. **NO-GO on AI in the core** (an antipattern).

## Risks

1. **Mono-causality (the main risk of the client's hypothesis).** If only the lawns/edges are fixed but the anti-icing-materials residue is not removed, the spring wet cleaning is not set up, and wheel washing at construction sites is not arranged — the cleanliness effect is partial → the reputational risk of "we spent the money but the dirt remained." Per the primary source, root cause #1 is anti-icing materials (~50%), not lawns.
2. **Without storm drains, lowering the lawn → flooding.** A sunken lawn = a rain garden only with a calculated infiltration/overflow; otherwise puddles and washout. Storm drainage (~RUB 44 bn) is capable of absorbing the entire scope — it is a separate mega-program.
3. **Cost is vendor-claim.** All prices, except the city hall budgets, are understated/Moscow-based; spread 3–6×. Lowering existing lawns (cutting + soil hauling, logistics, landfills) is often more expensive than the curb itself and is not reflected in price lists.
4. **"The client is late."** The city hall has already acknowledged the problem and is running a program (arterials only). If the client does not offer scale/control/coverage of courtyards and developers — there is nothing to "sell."
5. **Budget figures confuse the levels** (city vs region; maintenance vs construction vs equipment). RUB 24.3 (→21.7) bn is the region, not the city.
6. **Federal co-financing is being cut** (the national project was trimmed) — do not build a plan on growth in federal money.
7. **Biodrainage is poorly normalized in Russia**, the Siberian climate requires adaptation; the RUB 3,000–8,000/m² price is hypothetical, with a multifold spread.
8. **Pace.** At RUB 130 mn/year — tens to a hundred years. Without a multifold increase in financing or a tie to BKD, the program is not feasible within a foreseeable horizon.

Stream #4's ranking (#1 road wear) → primary source #1 = anti-icing materials. Stream #4's reading "SP does not forbid a lawn above the edge" → incorrect, this is a violation of the norm.

**Missed (accounted for in the report):** anti-icing materials as a standalone lever (the cost/effect of switching sand→chips not estimated); developers (zoning / land-use permits) as a cheap lever; storm drains as a separate mega-program (RUB 44 bn); recourse against utility companies not scaled; post-works maintenance almost unaddressed.

**Audit conclusion:** the hypothesis is confirmed as a real defect and publicly acknowledged by the city hall, but rejected as the "sole/main cause." **Go/No-Go: NO-GO until 5+ blockers are closed** — the budget holder/asset custodian, the post-works maintenance regulation, the inventory, the instrumental diagnosis (lawns vs anti-icing materials), the local estimates.
